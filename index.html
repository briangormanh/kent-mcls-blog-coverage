The one-line finding

The audience's strongest skill is interpreting (37%), ahead of translation (30%), and its single largest job title is sign-language interpreter. Its highest-volume search is "american sign language degree" (2,600/mo). The site's content is built almost entirely the other way around. The Q3 plan already started the pivot toward interpreting - the data says go further, and lead with ASL.

02

The four openings

Where audience demand and current coverage diverge most. The first two are net-new clusters; the second two sharpen work the Q3 plan already scoped. Each is a piece we would actually write.

Opening 01 · biggest single gap

Own the ASL interpreter pathway

The audience's largest identity and largest search term are both ASL, and the site meets them with nine soft "benefits of learning ASL" posts. There is no page on getting certified or building the career.

A piece we'd write
"How to Become a Certified ASL Interpreter: RID, NIC, and the Path in Ohio"
A step-by-step explainer: what RID (Registry of Interpreters for the Deaf) and the NIC (National Interpreter Certification) actually are, education requirements, the exam, and where Kent's ASL program fits. Anchor a small cluster: ASL interpreter salary, and ASL in medical vs. educational settings.
Why it wins  "ASL degree" 2,600/mo + "ASL interpreter certification" 720/mo. 25% of the audience already are sign-language interpreters, and they follow RID, the NAD, and Gallaudet - exactly the authorities this page would cite.
Vs. plan: extends the July interpreter-certification refresh into a dedicated ASL track instead of one shared page.
Opening 02 · high-intent cluster

A certification-led interpreter hub

Medical and court interpreting are proven converters for the program, but the searches are about getting credentialed, and the current posts are descriptive ("what is a court interpreter") rather than instructional.

A piece we'd write
"Medical Interpreter Certification: CCHI vs. CoreCNHI, Step by Step"
Reframe the medical and court interpreter posts around the credential path - boards, eligibility, exam, cost, timeline - then interlink them as a hub. The "translation vs. interpretation" post becomes the top-of-funnel entry point.
Why it wins  "medical interpreter certification" 317/mo plus "how to become a medical interpreter" 217/mo; "court interpreter training" 460/mo. Audience industries include hospital/health care, legal services, and judiciary.
Vs. plan: makes the August medical + court refreshes certification-first, not just longer.
Opening 03 · positioning

Be findable as "interpreting," not only "translation"

The degree reads as a translation program, but a real slice of the audience searches "interpreting master's degree" and "US interpreting schools." Those searchers may not recognize the program as for them.

A piece we'd write
"Translation or Interpreting? How One M.A. Prepares You for Both Careers"
A decision-guide that explicitly claims the interpreting query, explains how the coursework supports both paths, and routes readers to the right next step. Pair it with interpreting-aware anchor text from the main program page.
Why it wins  "interpreting master's degree" 152/mo, "US interpreting schools" 216/mo, "interpreter licensing" 375/mo - all currently landing on translation-framed pages the audience may bounce from.
Vs. plan: new framing task; complements the Q3 cannibalization and CTA work without overlapping it.
Opening 04 · decision content

Answer "is it worth it" with numbers

Two-thirds of the audience earns under $70k and is weighing a degree. Salary and "how much" content is the highest-intent, most AI-quotable format for this group - and it should cover interpreters, not just translators.

A piece we'd write
"How Much Do Interpreters and Translators Make? A 2026 Salary Guide by Specialization"
Structured ranges by setting and specialization (medical, legal, ASL, localization, freelance vs. staff), BLS outlook, and how an M.A. plus certification raises the ceiling. Clear methodology so AI answer engines quote it.
Why it wins  "How much" queries are a top trigger for AI Overviews and are exactly what a price-sensitive, ROI-driven audience searches before enrolling.
Vs. plan: broadens the August translator-salary post to include interpreting, the higher-demand half of the audience.

How this was built

Content inventory: a live crawl of the /mcls blog returned 116 unique post URLs, sorted by hand into the clusters above. Counts are approximate where a post spans two topics.

Audience: a SparkToro audience ("Aspiring translators and interpreters in the U.S.," report db1c8bc3a718) supplied the demographics, the search keywords with monthly volumes, the platform-affinity scores behind Figure 02, and the channel and podcast signals. Affinity is how far a group over-indexes against the average internet user; search volumes are U.S. monthly estimates.

Prioritization: the openings were weighed against the existing Q2 and Q3 strategies so the recommendations sharpen the current roadmap rather than compete with it. Search volumes move month to month and should be treated as direction, not a forecast.
